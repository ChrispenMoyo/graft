# 1. PURPOSE OF THIS DOCUMENT

This document defines the proposed business model for **GRAFT**.

GRAFT is a Zimbabwean marketplace designed to connect customers who need legitimate small jobs completed with nearby workers, informal contractors, independent tradespeople, and skilled individuals who can perform those jobs.

This document focuses specifically on:

- How GRAFT creates value
- Who GRAFT creates value for
- How the marketplace operates commercially
- How customers and workers interact economically
- Potential revenue streams
- The advantages and disadvantages of each monetization approach
- The recommended MVP monetization direction
- Future monetization opportunities
- Marketplace economics
- Commission considerations
- Payment-related business considerations
- Off-platform transaction risks
- Supply and demand considerations
- Business assumptions that still require validation

This is a **business and product foundation document**.

It does not define:

- Final EcoCash technical integration
- Database architecture
- APIs
- Payment API implementation
- Final legal structure
- Final commission percentages
- Final tax treatment
- Final regulatory requirements

Any area involving payments, financial regulation, tax, consumer protection, worker classification, identity processing, or money handling must be separately verified with appropriate Zimbabwean legal, financial, payment, and professional advisors.

# 2. BUSINESS MODEL SUMMARY

## 2.1 The GRAFT Business Model in One Sentence

> **GRAFT creates a trusted digital marketplace where customers post local jobs, suitable workers compete to perform them, customers choose the worker they prefer, and GRAFT earns revenue primarily from successful marketplace transactions.**

The central commercial model is based on facilitating successful transactions rather than charging people simply for joining the platform.

This is important because GRAFT's primary worker audience includes:

- Unemployed youths
- Informal workers
- Independent tradespeople
- Small contractors
- Skilled individuals seeking additional income
- People without formal companies
- Workers with inconsistent income

Charging these users a significant upfront fee before they receive work could create a major adoption barrier.

Therefore, the recommended initial direction is:

> **GRAFT should earn primarily when GRAFT successfully creates value through a completed marketplace transaction.**

This principle should guide the MVP business model.

# 3. THE CORE ECONOMIC PROBLEM GRAFT SOLVES

GRAFT operates between two groups with related but different problems.

## 3.1 Customer Problem

Customers need practical work completed.

For example:

- A leaking tap needs fixing
- A room needs painting
- Furniture needs moving
- A gate needs welding
- A garden needs maintenance
- A phone needs repair
- WiFi needs installation
- A vehicle needs assistance
- A house needs cleaning
- A small construction job needs labour

The customer often has a problem but does not immediately know:

- Who is available
- Who is nearby
- Who has the right skills
- Who can be trusted
- What a reasonable price is
- How long the worker will take to arrive
- Whether the worker has completed similar jobs
- Whether other customers had good experiences
- What happens if something goes wrong

Current alternatives often depend heavily on personal networks and informal communication.

GRAFT aims to reduce the effort required to move from:

> **"I need someone."**

to:

> **"I found a suitable worker, agreed on the job, and got it completed."**

---

## 3.2 Worker Problem

Many skilled people have the opposite problem.

They can perform work but do not have enough access to customers.

A worker may know how to:

- Fix plumbing problems
- Repair electrical systems
- Paint homes
- Weld gates
- Repair vehicles
- Install CCTV
- Install WiFi
- Clean properties
- Move furniture
- Perform general labour
- Repair phones
- Perform other legitimate practical services

However, having a skill does not automatically mean having access to consistent work.

Workers may depend on:

- Friends
- Family
- Referrals
- WhatsApp groups
- Facebook posts
- Existing customers
- Physical advertising
- Informal local networks

This can create an opportunity problem.

A skilled person may be available for work while, at the same time, someone nearby needs that exact skill.

The two people may simply not know about each other.

GRAFT aims to create the marketplace connection.

---

## 3.3 The GRAFT Opportunity

GRAFT creates value by reducing the gap between:

**Demand for practical work**

and

**Available people with practical skills.**

The core marketplace mechanism is:

```text
CUSTOMER HAS A JOB
        ↓
CUSTOMER POSTS JOB ON GRAFT
        ↓
GRAFT IDENTIFIES RELEVANT WORKERS
        ↓
SUITABLE WORKERS RECEIVE THE OPPORTUNITY
        ↓
WORKERS SUBMIT OFFERS / BIDS
        ↓
CUSTOMER COMPARES OPTIONS
        ↓
CUSTOMER SELECTS A WORKER
        ↓
PRICE AND JOB ARE CONFIRMED
        ↓
PAYMENT PROCESS FOLLOWS THE VALIDATED ECOCASH FLOW
        ↓
WORK IS PERFORMED
        ↓
JOB IS CONFIRMED / COMPLETED
        ↓
GRAFT RECORDS THE TRANSACTION
        ↓
REPUTATION AND TRUST DATA GROWS
```

The more successfully this transaction loop works, the more valuable the marketplace becomes.

# 4. GRAFT'S VALUE CREATION MODEL

GRAFT must create enough value for all major participants.

A marketplace cannot sustainably benefit only one side.

The model must work for:

1. Customers
2. Workers
3. GRAFT as the platform

---

## 4.1 Value for Customers

GRAFT should help customers solve the problem of finding and hiring someone for a small job.

The customer receives potential value through:

### Faster worker discovery

Instead of contacting multiple people individually, the customer can post a job and receive interest from suitable workers.

### Local relevance

Nearby workers may reduce:

- Waiting time
- Travel time
- Transport costs
- Job delays

However, proximity alone must not determine who gets hired.

### Worker comparison

Customers should be able to compare relevant information such as:

- Price
- Distance
- ETA
- Rating
- Number of completed jobs
- Verification status
- Skills
- Relevant experience

### Greater trust

GRAFT should provide structured trust signals that informal channels may not provide consistently.

Potential signals include:

- Phone verification
- Identity verification
- EcoCash verification
- Skill verification
- Ratings
- Completed jobs
- Reputation history
- Additional verification where appropriate

### Greater accountability

The marketplace creates a record of:

- The customer
- The worker
- The job
- The agreed price
- Job status
- Payment status
- Ratings
- Reports
- Disputes where applicable

### Structured transaction experience

GRAFT should provide more than simply showing a customer's phone number to a worker.

The value proposition is the structured process around the transaction.

---

## 4.2 Value for Workers

GRAFT should help workers turn practical skills into income opportunities.

The worker receives value through:

### Access to customers

Workers gain visibility beyond their existing personal network.

### Nearby opportunities

Location-aware matching may help workers discover jobs that are practical for them to reach.

### Ability to compete

A worker does not necessarily need to own a large formal business to participate.

Workers can compete using a combination of:

- Price
- Skills
- Reliability
- Availability
- Reputation
- Verification
- Experience

### Digital reputation

Each successfully completed job can potentially help the worker build a stronger marketplace reputation.

Over time, GRAFT may become part of a worker's digital professional identity.

### Repeat opportunities

Satisfied customers may potentially use the worker again where the product later supports repeat relationships and bookings.

### Reduced dependence on referrals

Referrals may remain important, but GRAFT can create an additional source of opportunities.

### Opportunity for new workers

GRAFT should not create a system where only workers with hundreds of previous jobs ever receive opportunities.

Verified new workers must have a realistic opportunity to enter the marketplace.

---

## 4.3 Value for GRAFT

GRAFT creates value when it successfully helps supply and demand meet.

Potential platform value includes:

- Transaction revenue
- A growing network of customers and workers
- Worker reputation data
- Marketplace performance data
- Demand information by location and category
- Increased trust infrastructure
- Future opportunities for premium services

However, GRAFT must not assume that user growth alone creates a sustainable business.

The important question is:

> **Are successful jobs being completed through the marketplace?**

A marketplace with 100,000 registered users but very few completed jobs is not necessarily healthy.

# 8. THE PRIMARY REVENUE MODEL

## 8.1 Recommended Direction: Transaction-Based Marketplace Revenue

The recommended MVP direction is:

> **GRAFT should investigate earning revenue primarily from successful paid jobs completed through the platform.**

This could involve a transaction-based platform fee.

The exact mechanics remain subject to validation.

Potential approaches include:

### Option A — Percentage-based fee

GRAFT receives a percentage of the completed transaction value.

Example only:

```text
Agreed Job Price
        ↓
Platform fee calculated
        ↓
GRAFT receives platform revenue
```

No percentage is being finalized in this document.

---

### Option B — Flat transaction fee

A fixed amount is charged per successful transaction.

Example:

```text
Every completed job
        ↓
Fixed GRAFT transaction fee
```

This may be easier to understand but can become unfair across different job values.

A very small job and a high-value job would receive the same fee unless multiple fee tiers are created.

---

### Option C — Percentage with Minimum and/or Maximum

A percentage fee may be subject to:

- A minimum platform fee
- A maximum platform fee
- Category-specific rules

This could protect GRAFT from extremely low-value transactions while preventing very high fees on larger jobs.

However, it adds complexity.

---

## 8.2 WHY Transaction-Based Revenue Is Recommended for MVP

### WHY

The marketplace creates its strongest value when a successful job is completed.

A transaction-based model aligns GRAFT's revenue with successful marketplace activity.

GRAFT earns when:

- A customer finds value
- A worker gets an opportunity
- A job proceeds successfully through the platform

This is more aligned with the marketplace's core purpose than charging workers before they have received any work.

### TRADE-OFF

Transaction-based revenue creates challenges:

- Small jobs may generate very little revenue
- Payment fees may reduce margins
- Users may try to transact outside GRAFT
- Payment integration may be operationally complex
- The exact payment and settlement flow must be validated

### RECOMMENDATION

Use transaction-based monetization as the **leading MVP business model**, subject to:

1. EcoCash capability validation
2. Payment fee analysis
3. Zimbabwean market research
4. Average job value research
5. Customer willingness-to-pay validation
6. Worker economics validation
7. Legal and regulatory verification

# 9. WHO SHOULD PAY THE PLATFORM FEE?

This is an important unresolved business decision.

There are several possible approaches.

---

## 9.1 Model A — Worker Pays the Platform Fee

Example:

```text
Customer agrees to pay $20
        ↓
Worker completes job
        ↓
GRAFT takes an agreed platform fee
        ↓
Worker receives the remaining amount
```

### Advantages

- Customer sees a simpler agreed job price
- GRAFT can market the worker's quoted amount directly
- The fee is connected to worker access to marketplace opportunities
- GRAFT only earns when the worker earns

### Disadvantages

- Workers may feel punished for using the platform
- The model could discourage unemployed workers
- Workers may increase their bids to recover the fee
- Workers may attempt to move customers off-platform

### Assessment

This model is possible, but GRAFT must be careful because workers are a critical supply side.

If workers do not see enough value, the marketplace cannot function.

---

## 9.2 Model B — Customer Pays a Service Fee

Example:

```text
Worker's agreed price: $20
GRAFT service fee: additional amount
Total customer payment: higher than $20
```

### Advantages

- Worker can receive the full agreed service price
- The worker's earnings are easier to explain
- GRAFT's fee can be clearly separated from the service price

### Disadvantages

- Customers may perceive GRAFT as making the job more expensive
- Very small jobs may become uneconomical
- Users may compare the total platform cost with arranging the job privately

### Assessment

This model is transparent but must be carefully tested for price sensitivity.

---

## 9.3 Model C — Fee Built Into the Transaction Structure

The platform may structure the transaction so that the marketplace fee is incorporated into the overall payment flow.

For example:

```text
Worker provides offer
        ↓
Customer sees final amount according to GRAFT pricing rules
        ↓
Platform calculates revenue internally
```

### Advantages

- Can simplify the customer experience
- Can simplify presentation of the total transaction
- Platform economics can remain consistent

### Disadvantages

- Must not reduce transparency
- Users should understand what they are paying
- Payment, consumer, and disclosure requirements must be verified

---

## 9.4 Model D — Split Fee

Both parties contribute to the cost of using the marketplace.

For example:

- Customer pays a small service fee
- Worker contributes a small transaction fee

### Advantages

- Revenue burden is shared
- No single party bears the entire marketplace cost

### Disadvantages

- More complicated to explain
- Both sides may feel they are being charged
- Adds pricing complexity for MVP

### Assessment

Not recommended as the first model unless market validation demonstrates a clear advantage.

---

# 10. RECOMMENDED MVP FEE DIRECTION

The current recommended direction is:

> **Use one simple transaction-based platform fee model for MVP, with the exact fee payer and percentage to be validated before launch.**

The strongest principle is simplicity.

GRAFT should avoid launching with:

- Multiple subscription plans
- Different complicated pricing formulas
- Premium worker tiers
- Promoted listing packages
- Complex dynamic marketplace fees
- Multiple payment rails

The MVP should answer one business question first:

> **Can GRAFT create enough value in a completed transaction for users to accept a reasonable marketplace fee?**

# 11. COMMISSION PERCENTAGE

## 11.1 No Final Percentage Is Approved

This document does **not** recommend hard-coding a specific commission percentage yet.

The Phase 1 source material explicitly identifies the exact platform fee as an important open business question.

Therefore:

> **No final GRAFT commission percentage should be treated as a product decision until market, payment, operational, and financial validation has been completed.**

---

## 11.2 Why a Percentage Cannot Be Chosen Arbitrarily

A sustainable commission depends on:

- Average job value
- Frequency of small jobs
- EcoCash transaction costs
- Refund costs
- Dispute costs
- KYC costs
- Customer support costs
- Fraud losses
- Worker acquisition costs
- Customer acquisition costs
- Platform operating costs
- User willingness to pay
- Competitive alternatives
- Off-platform transaction behaviour

For example, a commission that appears reasonable on a large construction-related job may be uneconomical on a very small household job.

The opposite can also be true.

A low commission may appear attractive to users but fail to support:

- Payment operations
- Customer support
- KYC review
- Fraud monitoring
- Dispute handling
- Product development
- Marketplace operations

---

## 11.3 Required Commission Validation

Before finalizing the fee, GRAFT should test:

### Question 1

What is the average expected value of initial jobs?

### Question 2

How much revenue does GRAFT generate at different commission levels?

### Question 3

How much of that revenue remains after payment and operating costs?

### Question 4

At what fee level do workers begin preferring to move transactions outside GRAFT?

### Question 5

At what fee level do customers consider the platform too expensive?

### Question 6

Do different categories have significantly different economics?

### Question 7

Does GRAFT need a minimum transaction fee?

### Question 8

Can the validated EcoCash payment model support the proposed commission structure?

# 12. SECONDARY MVP REVENUE OPTIONS

The following models should be considered but are not currently recommended as the primary MVP monetization model.

---

## 12.1 Worker Registration Fee

Workers pay to join GRAFT.

### WHY IT MAY WORK

Could generate early revenue.

### TRADE-OFF

Creates friction before the worker has received value.

This is particularly problematic because GRAFT wants to attract:

- Unemployed youths
- Informal workers
- Workers with inconsistent income

### RECOMMENDATION

**Do not use as a core MVP requirement.**

Worker registration should not become a paywall that prevents skilled people from entering the marketplace.

---

## 12.2 Worker Subscription

Workers pay monthly for platform access.

### Potential Advantages

- Predictable recurring revenue
- Simple recurring commercial relationship

### Risks

- Workers may pay without receiving enough jobs
- New workers may be unwilling to pay before seeing value
- Low marketplace liquidity makes subscriptions difficult to justify
- Creates pressure on GRAFT to promise opportunities it may not control

### RECOMMENDATION

**Not recommended for MVP.**

Potential future option only after GRAFT has proven recurring worker value.

---

## 12.3 Customer Subscription

Customers pay monthly for marketplace access.

### Potential Use Cases

Could eventually be relevant for:

- Property managers
- Landlords
- Businesses
- Frequent users

### MVP Assessment

Most ordinary customers may use GRAFT only when they need a job completed.

A monthly subscription may therefore create unnecessary friction.

### RECOMMENDATION

**Not recommended for ordinary consumer MVP users.**

---

## 12.4 Lead Generation Fee

Workers pay to access customer leads.

### Advantages

Could create revenue before a completed job.

### Risks

Workers may pay for opportunities that do not convert into work.

This could damage trust in the platform.

It also creates a different marketplace model from the preferred outcome-based transaction model.

### RECOMMENDATION

**Do not use for MVP unless later validation strongly supports it.**

# 14. GROSS TRANSACTION VALUE

A key business metric for GRAFT is the total value of jobs flowing through the marketplace.

This can be referred to as:

> **Gross Transaction Value (GTV)**

Conceptually:

```text
JOB 1 VALUE
+
JOB 2 VALUE
+
JOB 3 VALUE
+
...
=
TOTAL MARKETPLACE TRANSACTION VALUE
```

GRAFT revenue is not automatically equal to GTV.

For example:

```text
Total value of completed jobs
        ↓
GTV
        ↓
Applicable platform revenue model
        ↓
GRAFT revenue
```

This distinction is important.

A marketplace may process significant transaction value while retaining only a small percentage as platform revenue.

# 16. SMALL-JOB ECONOMICS RISK

GRAFT is focused heavily on small jobs.

This creates an important business challenge.

A small job may generate:

- A small commission
- A small service fee

But the platform may still incur:

- Payment costs
- Support costs
- Fraud monitoring
- Infrastructure costs
- Notification costs
- KYC-related costs
- Dispute costs

Therefore, GRAFT must validate whether extremely low-value jobs can support the same transaction process as larger jobs.

## WHY THIS MATTERS

If GRAFT treats every job identically, very small jobs may become commercially inefficient.

## TRADE-OFF

Introducing minimum fees or minimum job values can make the platform less accessible.

## RECOMMENDATION

Do not finalize a minimum job value in Phase 1.

Instead, mark the following for validation:

> **What is the minimum economically viable transaction value for GRAFT under the actual EcoCash, operational, and support cost structure?**

The business model depends significantly on one unanswered question:

> **How will money actually move through the GRAFT marketplace using the validated EcoCash capabilities available at launch?**

Therefore, the final business model must remain compatible with actual payment reality.

## REQUIRES VERIFICATION

GRAFT must establish:

- How payment initiation works
- How payment confirmation works
- Whether transaction status can be reliably verified
- Whether marketplace settlement is supported
- Whether funds can be held
- Whether funds can be split
- How worker payouts work
- How refunds work
- What fees apply
- What transaction limits apply
- What compliance obligations apply
- Whether a separate authorized payment provider is required

# 20. CANCELLATIONS, REFUNDS AND BUSINESS COST

The business model must account for failed transactions.

Potential situations include:

- Customer cancels before worker selection
- Worker withdraws
- Worker does not arrive
- Customer is unavailable
- Customer cancels after payment
- Worker cancels after payment
- Job cannot be completed
- Job is partially completed
- Customer disputes the work
- Worker disputes non-payment
- Payment fails
- Duplicate payment occurs
- Wrong amount is paid

Each situation may affect:

- Customer trust
- Worker earnings
- GRAFT revenue
- Payment costs
- Support costs
- Fraud risk

The business model must not simply assume:

> Every posted job becomes a completed transaction.

GRAFT should measure:

- Posted jobs
- Jobs receiving bids
- Jobs with selected workers
- Jobs with confirmed payments
- Jobs started
- Jobs completed
- Jobs successfully settled
- Jobs cancelled
- Jobs disputed

The difference between these stages is important to marketplace economics.

---

# 21. DISPUTES AND REVENUE

A platform commission model creates an important question:

> **When does GRAFT officially earn revenue from a transaction?**

Potential possibilities include:

- At payment initiation
- At payment confirmation
- At work completion
- After the dispute period
- After settlement

The answer depends on the actual payment, accounting, and legal model.

### RECOMMENDATION

For business measurement purposes, distinguish clearly between:

- Expected revenue
- Payment received
- Revenue pending dispute resolution
- Successfully recognized marketplace revenue

The final accounting treatment requires professional verification.

# 22. OFF-PLATFORM TRANSACTION RISK

One of GRAFT's largest business risks is:

> **Customer and worker meet through GRAFT, exchange contact details, and complete future transactions outside the platform.**

This is commonly referred to as disintermediation or off-platform leakage.

Example:

```text
Customer posts job on GRAFT
        ↓
Worker discovers customer through GRAFT
        ↓
Customer and worker communicate
        ↓
They bypass GRAFT payment and transaction flow
        ↓
GRAFT receives no transaction revenue
```

This can weaken the business model.

---

# 23. WHY USERS MAY GO OFF-PLATFORM

Users may bypass GRAFT because:

- They want to avoid fees
- They already trust each other
- Payment outside the platform feels easier
- GRAFT does not provide enough additional value
- The job requires further negotiation
- Users want direct repeat arrangements

GRAFT should not assume that technology alone can completely prevent this behaviour.

---

# 24. RECOMMENDED APPROACH TO OFF-PLATFORM RISK

The strongest defence is not simply restricting users.

The strongest defence is creating enough value inside GRAFT.

Users should have reasons to continue using the platform.

Potential reasons include:

- Verification
- Reputation
- Structured job records
- Payment tracking
- Dispute support
- Ratings
- Trust signals
- Convenient job posting
- Worker discovery
- Marketplace history
- Repeat transaction convenience

## PRODUCT PRINCIPLE

> **GRAFT should make staying on the platform more valuable than bypassing it.**

### TRADE-OFF

Overly restrictive communication controls may frustrate legitimate users.

### RECOMMENDATION

Do not rely exclusively on blocking behaviour.

Prioritize genuine marketplace value.

Communication and privacy rules can later be defined in the product requirements phase.

- Referral programmes

- Customer referrals

The specific acquisition strategy is outside this document.

Therefore, repeat usage is strategically valuable.

# 27. THE CHICKEN-AND-EGG PROBLEM

GRAFT has a fundamental marketplace problem.

Customers need enough available workers to believe GRAFT is useful.

Workers need enough job opportunities to believe GRAFT is worth using.

This creates the classic marketplace loop:

```text
NO WORKERS
    ↓
CUSTOMERS RECEIVE NO RESPONSES
    ↓
CUSTOMERS LEAVE
    ↓
FEWER JOBS
    ↓
WORKERS LEAVE
```

GRAFT must deliberately manage this problem.

---

# 28. RECOMMENDED MARKETPLACE LAUNCH MODEL

## WHY

A nationwide launch may spread demand and supply too thinly.

For example:

- 100 workers spread across the entire country may not help a customer in one specific location.
- 1,000 workers across many cities may still produce poor local job liquidity.

Marketplace usefulness is local.

A customer needs suitable workers available in the relevant area.

### RECOMMENDATION

Launch in a concentrated geographic market.

The exact launch city remains an open question requiring validation.

Possible locations identified in the Phase 1 requirements include:

- Harare
- Bulawayo
- Gweru
- Mutare
- Other suitable urban or peri-urban areas

The launch decision should be based on evidence rather than assumption.

Factors should include:

- Population density
- Customer demand
- Worker supply
- Smartphone access
- Network conditions
- Operational ability
- Payment feasibility
- KYC feasibility
- Initial category supply

---

# 29. MARKETPLACE LIQUIDITY

The most important early business challenge is not simply registration.

It is marketplace liquidity.

GRAFT must determine whether:

- Customers post jobs
- Jobs receive bids
- Bids are relevant
- Customers select workers
- Workers accept the selection
- Jobs actually start
- Jobs complete successfully

A useful liquidity funnel is:

```text
REGISTERED CUSTOMERS
        ↓
ACTIVE CUSTOMERS
        ↓
JOBS POSTED
        ↓
JOBS RECEIVING BIDS
        ↓
JOBS WITH WORKER SELECTED
        ↓
JOBS STARTED
        ↓
JOBS COMPLETED
        ↓
SUCCESSFUL PAID TRANSACTIONS
```

The marketplace should identify where users drop out.

For example:

If many jobs are posted but few receive bids, the supply or matching model may be failing.

If many bids are received but few workers are selected, bid quality or customer confidence may be failing.

If workers are selected but many jobs are cancelled, pricing, trust, scheduling, or payment problems may exist.

---

# 30. NEW WORKER FAIRNESS AS A BUSINESS REQUIREMENT

New workers are strategically important.

If GRAFT only rewards workers with:

- Many previous jobs
- Many ratings
- Long platform history

then the marketplace may become closed to newcomers.

This creates a business problem.

The platform may lose:

- New skilled workers
- Geographic expansion capacity
- Category expansion capacity
- Worker diversity

## RECOMMENDED PRINCIPLE

A verified new worker with relevant skills should receive a reasonable opportunity to be discovered.

This does not mean guaranteeing work.

It means GRAFT should avoid a permanent winner-takes-all marketplace.

Potential future mechanisms may include:

- Controlled exposure for new verified workers
- Rotation
- Skill relevance
- Geographic relevance
- Availability
- Verification
- Fair ranking logic

---

# 31. PRICE COMPETITION AND THE RACE-TO-THE-BOTTOM RISK

Bidding can create customer choice.

However, unrestricted price competition can create problems.

Workers may underbid each other simply to win.

This can result in:

- Unsustainable earnings
- Lower-quality work
- Pressure to cut corners
- Workers later attempting to increase prices
- Worker dissatisfaction

Therefore, GRAFT should not design its comparison experience around:

> **Lowest price wins.**

Customers should compare multiple signals.

The intended model is:

> **Price + Distance + ETA + Trust + Verification + Reputation + Experience**

### RECOMMENDATION

GRAFT should make quality visible enough that workers can compete on more than price.

---

# 32. CATEGORY ECONOMICS

Different job categories may have different business characteristics.

For example:

- A quick gardening task may have a low transaction value
- A specialist electrical job may require greater trust
- A mobile mechanic may require travel
- A photographer may have a higher job value
- Construction assistance may involve longer jobs

Therefore, GRAFT should not assume that every category will have identical economics.

Future analysis may eventually consider:

- Average job value by category
- Completion rate by category
- Dispute rate by category
- Repeat usage by category
- Average worker travel distance
- Worker supply
- Customer demand
- Payment economics
- Safety risk

For MVP, GRAFT should avoid adding category-specific commercial complexity too early.

# 39. BUSINESS RISKS

## 39.1 Insufficient Marketplace Liquidity

### Risk

Customers post jobs but receive no useful bids.

### Impact

Customers stop trusting the marketplace.

### Recommendation

Launch geographically in a concentrated market and recruit relevant worker supply.

---

## 39.2 Worker Oversupply

### Risk

Too many workers join but there are insufficient jobs.

### Impact

Workers become inactive or dissatisfied.

### Recommendation

Monitor jobs-per-active-worker and expand supply deliberately.

---

## 39.3 Low Transaction Value

### Risk

Small jobs generate insufficient revenue.

### Impact

GRAFT may facilitate activity without sustainable economics.

### Recommendation

Validate average job values and unit economics before finalizing commission rules.

---

## 39.4 Payment Capability Risk

### Risk

The desired marketplace payment model may not be technically or commercially supported by available EcoCash capabilities.

### Impact

The core transaction model may need adjustment.

### Recommendation

Validate payment capabilities before final payment architecture or public product promises.

---

## 39.5 Off-Platform Leakage

### Risk

Users bypass GRAFT after meeting.

### Impact

Reduced platform revenue and visibility.

### Recommendation

Create genuine on-platform value rather than relying only on restrictions.

---

## 39.6 Race to the Bottom

### Risk

Workers underbid excessively.

### Impact

Poor worker economics and potentially poor quality.

### Recommendation

Present trust and quality signals alongside price.

---

## 39.7 Trust Failure

### Risk

A serious fraud or safety incident damages the GRAFT brand.

### Impact

Customer and worker confidence may decline rapidly.

### Recommendation

Treat verification, reporting, safety, and moderation as core business infrastructure.

---

## 39.8 High Operational Cost

### Risk

Manual KYC, support, fraud review, and disputes become expensive.

### Impact

Low-margin transactions may not cover operating costs.

### Recommendation

Measure operational cost per completed transaction and avoid introducing high-cost processes without justification.

4. Strong verification will increase trust.

12. GRAFT can acquire sufficient supply in a concentrated launch market.
13. GRAFT can acquire enough demand to keep workers engaged.
14. The validated EcoCash ecosystem can support a workable marketplace transaction flow.
15. The operational cost of KYC, disputes, and support can be managed sustainably.

These are assumptions, not confirmed facts.

# 44. FINAL BUSINESS MODEL RECOMMENDATION

The recommended Phase 1 business model for GRAFT is:

> **Build a focused Zimbabwean marketplace that earns primarily when it successfully facilitates valuable completed jobs, rather than charging workers heavily before they receive opportunities.**

The MVP should focus on proving the following commercial loop:

```text
CUSTOMER NEEDS A JOB DONE
        ↓
CUSTOMER POSTS ON GRAFT
        ↓
SUITABLE NEARBY WORKERS DISCOVER THE JOB
        ↓
WORKERS SUBMIT BIDS
        ↓
CUSTOMER COMPARES OPTIONS
        ↓
CUSTOMER SELECTS A WORKER
        ↓
PRICE IS AGREED
        ↓
VALIDATED ECOCASH PAYMENT FLOW
        ↓
WORK IS COMPLETED
        ↓
CUSTOMER CONFIRMS OR DISPUTES
        ↓
TRANSACTION IS SUCCESSFULLY CONCLUDED
        ↓
GRAFT EARNS ACCORDING TO THE VALIDATED PLATFORM FEE MODEL
        ↓
BOTH SIDES BUILD REPUTATION
```

The most important strategic principle is:

> **GRAFT must prove marketplace value before adding monetization complexity.**

The first goal is not to create many revenue streams.

The first goal is to prove that GRAFT can reliably create successful transactions between:

- A real customer
- A trusted suitable worker
- A legitimate local job
- A workable agreed price
- A reliable payment process
- A successful job outcome

If GRAFT can consistently achieve that outcome, it creates the foundation for a sustainable Zimbabwean marketplace.

# 45. BUSINESS MODEL PRINCIPLES

Future business decisions for GRAFT should follow these principles:

1. **Revenue should follow real value creation.**
2. **Do not punish workers before they have received value.**
3. **Keep MVP pricing simple and understandable.**
4. **Do not finalize commission percentages without real economic validation.**
5. **Small-job economics must be measured, not assumed.**
6. **Marketplace liquidity is more important than vanity registration numbers.**
7. **Completed jobs matter more than app downloads.**
8. **Trust and safety are business infrastructure, not optional extras.**

11. **The marketplace should create enough value to discourage off-platform leakage.**
12. **Do not build future monetization features before the core GRAFT transaction loop is proven.**
13. **EcoCash capabilities must be verified before final payment promises are made.**
14. **Zimbabwe-specific market realities must guide commercial decisions.**
15. **Every monetization feature must improve or support the marketplace without damaging trust.**
