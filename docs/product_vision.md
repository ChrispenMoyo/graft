This document defines the long-term product vision and strategic direction for **GRAFT**.

Its purpose is to answer:

- Why GRAFT should exist
- What long-term problem GRAFT is intended to solve
- Who GRAFT is being built for
- What future GRAFT is trying to create
- What makes the product specifically relevant to Zimbabwe
- What core value GRAFT should provide to customers
- What core value GRAFT should provide to workers
- How GRAFT should be positioned
- What GRAFT should become over time
- What GRAFT should not become
- What principles should guide future product decisions
- How the long-term vision should remain connected to a focused MVP

This is a strategic product document.

It is **not**:

- A screen-by-screen specification
- A UI design document
- A technical architecture document
- A database specification
- An API specification
- A final payment integration specification
- A legal opinion
- A final marketing plan

Detailed product requirements, UX flows, screens, technical architecture, and implementation decisions belong in later phases.

> **What is GRAFT trying to become, and why should every future product decision move in that direction?**

# 2. THE GRAFT VISION IN ONE SENTENCE

> **To become Zimbabwe's trusted digital marketplace for getting legitimate small jobs done while creating accessible income opportunities for skilled, unemployed, underemployed, informal, and independent workers.**

This vision has two equally important sides:

```text
CUSTOMER VALUE
"Help me get something done."

                +
                GRAFT

                +
WORKER VALUE
"Help my skills find work."
```

GRAFT should not solve only one side.

A successful marketplace must create meaningful value for both:

- People who need legitimate work done
- People who have skills and need access to work

---

# 3. THE LONG-TERM GRAFT VISION

The long-term opportunity is to build a trusted digital infrastructure layer for Zimbabwe's informal and small-service economy.

Today, a person who needs a small job completed may depend on:

- Friends and family
- Personal referrals
- WhatsApp groups
- Facebook groups
- Facebook Marketplace
- Neighbours
- Individual contractors
- Local noticeboards
- Google or social media searches
- Informal word of mouth

A person with useful skills may depend on:

- Personal contacts
- Referrals
- Walking around looking for work
- WhatsApp status updates
- Facebook posts
- Existing customers
- Informal networks

These methods can work, but they are fragmented.

GRAFT aims to create a more structured connection.

The long-term vision is that a customer should eventually be able to open GRAFT for almost any legitimate small task and answer:

> **Who nearby can do this, can I trust them, what will they charge, and how do I get the job completed?**

Examples include:

- “I need someone to fix my leaking tap.”
- “I need someone to mow my yard.”
- “I need someone to move a couch.”
- “I need a welder for a small gate repair.”
- “I need someone to install WiFi.”
- “I need someone to paint one room.”
- “I need a mechanic to come to my car.”
- “I need someone to repair my washing machine.”
- “I need two people to help move furniture.”

For workers, the long-term vision is:

> **A person's practical skills should not remain invisible simply because they do not have a formal company, website, large advertising budget, or powerful personal network.**

GRAFT should help make legitimate skills discoverable.

# 4. WHY GRAFT SHOULD EXIST

## 4.1 The Customer Problem

People frequently need small jobs completed but may struggle to find trustworthy workers quickly.

The problem is not always that workers do not exist.

Often, the problem is:

> **The customer does not know who is available, who is suitable, who can be trusted, how much the job should cost, or how to compare the available options.**

Informal channels may create additional problems:

- No consistent identity verification
- Unclear pricing
- Limited availability information
- No standardized reputation system
- Difficult comparison between workers
- Poor visibility into distance
- No structured job lifecycle
- Limited accountability
- Difficult dispute handling
- Higher risk when inviting unknown people into homes
- Fragmented communication and payment arrangements

The customer journey is often:

```text
I HAVE A PROBLEM
        ↓
WHO DO I ASK?
        ↓
ASK FRIENDS / WHATSAPP / FACEBOOK
        ↓
WAIT FOR RESPONSES
        ↓
TRY TO DECIDE WHO TO TRUST
        ↓
CALL / MESSAGE MULTIPLE PEOPLE
        ↓
NEGOTIATE
        ↓
HOPE THE PERSON ARRIVES
        ↓
HOPE THE WORK IS DONE PROPERLY
```

GRAFT should make this journey more structured.

The intended journey is:

```text
I HAVE A PROBLEM
        ↓
OPEN GRAFT
        ↓
POST THE JOB
        ↓
SUITABLE WORKERS DISCOVER IT
        ↓
COMPARE OFFERS
        ↓
CHECK RELEVANT TRUST SIGNALS
        ↓
CHOOSE A WORKER
        ↓
TRACK JOB PROGRESS
        ↓
COMPLETE THE JOB
        ↓
BUILD REPUTATION
```

The customer does not need another place where people merely advertise themselves.

The customer needs a reliable path from:

> **"I need something done."**

to:

> **"The right person was found and the job was completed."**

---

## 4.2 The Worker Problem

Zimbabwe has many people with useful practical skills who may struggle to access consistent work.

This includes:

- Unemployed youths
- Underemployed people
- Informal tradespeople
- Independent contractors
- Skilled individuals working part-time
- People starting small service businesses
- Workers relying heavily on referrals
- Workers without formal companies or websites

Their problem is often not simply:

> **"I have no skill."**

It may be:

> **"I have a skill, but I do not consistently know where the next customer will come from."**

Common worker problems include:

- Limited customer reach
- Unpredictable work
- Dependence on personal networks
- Difficulty proving trustworthiness
- Difficulty competing with established contractors
- Time spent searching for opportunities
- Lack of a structured digital reputation
- Difficulty finding customers outside an immediate network
- Limited visibility despite having relevant skills

GRAFT should help move workers from:

> **Invisible skill**

toward:

> **Discoverable skill + verified identity + completed job history + reputation.**

The worker-side promise is:

> **Your skills can find work near you.**

---

## 4.3 The Marketplace Problem

The fundamental marketplace problem is that supply and demand may already exist close to each other without being effectively connected.

```text
CUSTOMER
Needs a plumber
        │
        │ Does not know who is available
        ▼
      GRAFT
        ▲
        │ Has plumbing skills
        │
WORKER
Needs work
```

GRAFT is intended to create the connection through:

> **Location + Skills + Trust + Availability + Bidding + Payment**

The product should reduce the friction between:

```text
A REAL CUSTOMER NEED
        ↓
A SUITABLE WORKER
        ↓
A CLEAR AGREEMENT
        ↓
A COMPLETED JOB
```

# 5. THE CORE PRODUCT PROMISE

The customer-side promise is:

> **Post a small job, find trusted people nearby, compare their offers, choose who you want, and get the job done.**

The worker-side promise is:

> **Your skills can find work near you.**

The marketplace promise is:

> **Create a structured and trustworthy path between a legitimate local need and a suitable person who can complete it.**

These promises should guide the product more than feature count.

# 6. THE FUNDAMENTAL QUESTIONS GRAFT MUST SOLVE

GRAFT exists to make two interactions dramatically easier.

## For Customers

> **“I need something done. Who nearby can do it, can I trust them, what will they charge, and can I safely get the job completed?”**

This contains five core product questions:

1. Who can do it?
2. Who is nearby or realistically available?
3. Can I trust them?
4. What will they charge?
5. Can the work move safely toward completion?

## For Workers

> **“I have a skill. Where can I find someone nearby who needs it and pay me fairly?”**

This contains three core product questions:

1. Where are the relevant opportunities?
2. How do I compete fairly for them?
3. Can I build a reputation that helps me access future work?

If GRAFT solves these two sets of questions reliably, the product has a reason to exist.

# 8. THE LONG-TERM PRODUCT AMBITION

The long-term ambition is not merely to build an application containing service categories.

The deeper ambition is to make GRAFT a trusted system through which:

- Legitimate small jobs can become visible
- Suitable workers can become discoverable
- Workers can build digital reputations
- Customers can compare meaningful options
- Local distance can reduce unnecessary travel
- Availability can improve response speed
- Identity and verification can improve trust
- Completed work can create a stronger history of reliability
- Payment can become more structured
- Repeated successful transactions can strengthen the marketplace

Over time, GRAFT could become a trusted layer between informal demand and informal or independent supply.

This means the long-term value is not only:

> **"Find me a worker."**

It is also:

> **"Help the marketplace know who has successfully completed work and who can be trusted based on meaningful platform signals."**

---

# 9. GRAFT AS TRUSTED MARKETPLACE INFRASTRUCTURE

Trust is central to the long-term product vision.

GRAFT should not assume that simply displaying a worker's phone number is enough.

The product vision includes building trust through appropriate signals such as:

- Phone verification
- Identity verification
- EcoCash verification where feasible
- Skill evidence where appropriate
- Completed job history
- Ratings
- Reviews or structured feedback
- Reputation milestones
- Reporting
- Blocking
- Dispute handling
- Platform moderation
- Clear job history

The exact implementation of these systems requires separate requirements and validation.

The vision principle is:

> **Trust should become stronger through successful marketplace participation, not through unstructured claims alone.**

---

# 10. THE ROLE OF LOCATION IN THE VISION

GRAFT is intended to be hyperlocal.

Location matters because local services often require a person to physically travel to a customer, property, vehicle, or worksite.

Location can help answer:

- Who is realistically nearby?
- Who can reach the job?
- How far away is the worker?
- What is the estimated arrival time?
- Which workers operate within the relevant service area?

However:

> **The closest worker should not automatically win.**

The long-term vision is not a marketplace based on:

```text
FIND THE CLOSEST PERSON
        ↓
AUTOMATICALLY ASSIGN THEM
```

Instead:

```text
REQUIRED SKILL
        +
LOCATION
        +
AVAILABILITY
        +
TRUST
        +
QUALITY
        +
FAIRNESS
        ↓
RELEVANT OPTIONS
        ↓
CUSTOMER CAN COMPARE AND CHOOSE
```

Location is therefore a major marketplace input, not the only decision.

---

# 11. THE ROLE OF BIDDING IN THE VISION

Bidding is intended to give workers an opportunity to compete for relevant jobs and give customers meaningful options.

The worker says:

> **"I can do this job, and here is my offer."**

The customer can then compare:

- Proposed price
- Distance
- ETA
- Rating
- Completed jobs
- Verification
- Skills
- Relevant experience
- Reliability signals where appropriate

The product vision should avoid assuming:

> **Lowest price = best worker**

or:

> **Closest worker = best worker**

The intended marketplace balance is:

> **PRICE + DISTANCE + TRUST + AVAILABILITY + QUALITY**

Bidding should help create customer choice while allowing workers to compete based on more than personal connections.

---

# 12. CUSTOMER CONTROL AS A PRODUCT PRINCIPLE

GRAFT may eventually provide recommendations.

However, the vision is that customers should generally retain meaningful control over whom they hire.

The platform should help the customer make an informed decision rather than hiding all marketplace choice behind an automatic assignment.

GRAFT should structure information clearly enough for the customer to answer:

- Is this person relevant to my job?
- Can this person reasonably reach me?
- What is the proposed price?
- What is their estimated arrival?
- What trust signals do they have?
- What experience or completed work signals do they have?

The recommendation is:

> **Assist the decision. Do not unnecessarily remove the customer's ability to decide.**

---

# 13. WORKER OPPORTUNITY AND FAIRNESS

A major part of the GRAFT vision is creating accessible opportunities for people with useful skills.

This means the product should not evolve into a system where only workers who already have:

- Many reviews
- Long platform histories
- Large numbers of completed jobs
- Existing popularity

receive all meaningful opportunities.

A new worker may be:

- Properly verified
- Skilled
- Available
- Nearby
- Reasonably priced

but have no completed GRAFT jobs.

The vision therefore includes marketplace fairness.

The principle is:

> **GRAFT should give relevant verified new workers a genuine opportunity to be discovered and compete without guaranteeing them work.**

Potential mechanisms may later include:

- Verification-based visibility
- New-worker discovery opportunities
- Geographic rotation
- Fair ranking
- Skill relevance
- Controlled exposure
- Quality-based recommendations

The exact mechanism should be defined later.

The strategic principle should remain:

> **Opportunity should not permanently belong only to the people who already have it.**

---

# 14. WHY GRAFT IS SPECIFIC TO ZIMBABWE

GRAFT should be designed for Zimbabwe from the beginning.

Zimbabwe is not intended to be a generic market added through later localization.

The product vision is shaped by:

- Local payment behaviour
- EcoCash as the initial payment direction
- Informal and independent work
- The importance of referrals
- Local trust challenges
- Practical small-job demand
- Smartphone and affordable Android usage
- Slow or unstable connectivity
- Mobile data costs
- The need for low-bandwidth product behaviour
- Zimbabwe-specific identity and KYC considerations
- Local geographic and operational realities

The principle is:

> **Do not build a generic marketplace and later attempt to make it Zimbabwean. Build GRAFT around Zimbabwean conditions from day one.**

# 15. LOW-BANDWIDTH-FIRST PRODUCT VISION

The product vision must remain compatible with the reality that users may experience:

- Slow mobile networks
- Expensive data
- Unstable connectivity
- Older Android phones
- Limited RAM
- Limited storage
- Battery constraints

GRAFT should therefore be guided by:

> **Works even when the internet is bad.**

This means future product decisions should favour:

- Lightweight experiences
- Compressed images
- Minimal unnecessary network requests
- Efficient APIs
- Caching
- Retry mechanisms
- Connection-state awareness
- Graceful failures
- Efficient notifications
- Efficient GPS usage
- Minimal unnecessary background processing

The product should not assume:

> **The user always has fast internet.**

Instead:

> **The user may lose connectivity at any moment.**

This is not a technical afterthought.

It is part of the GRAFT product identity.

# 17. ECOCASH-FIRST PRODUCT DIRECTION

The initial payment direction is:

# ECOCASH ONLY

The purpose is to focus the early product around a payment ecosystem relevant to the intended Zimbabwean market.

The MVP should not depend on:

- Credit cards
- PayPal
- Stripe
- Cryptocurrency
- Generic wallets
- Cash as an official platform payment method

However, an important product constraint remains:

> **GRAFT must not assume EcoCash capabilities that have not been validated.**

Before implementation, the relevant capabilities must be established, including:

- Available APIs
- Merchant capabilities
- Payment initiation
- Payment confirmation
- Transaction references
- Refund capability
- Settlement
- Payout mechanisms
- Compliance requirements
- Fees
- Limits
- Integration requirements

The long-term vision is:

> **Make payment part of the structured marketplace experience where technically, commercially, and legally feasible.**

The exact payment architecture remains subject to validation.

# 19. WHAT GRAFT SHOULD NOT BECOME

A clear vision also requires clear boundaries.

GRAFT should not become:

- A general social network
- A traditional corporate employment platform
- A classifieds website
- A Facebook Marketplace clone
- A generic freelancer platform
- A cleaning-only platform
- A payment wallet
- A simple directory of worker phone numbers
- A platform that automatically sends every job to every worker
- A feature-heavy application with no proven marketplace transaction loop

The core identity should remain:

> **Local task marketplace + trusted workers + targeted matching + bidding + location + structured transaction.**

- Discovery
- Trust
- Matching
- Choice
- Transaction completion
- Marketplace quality
- Worker opportunity

# 26. PRODUCT GROWTH DIRECTION

The recommended strategic growth sequence is:

## Step 1 — Concentrate

Launch in one carefully selected geographic market.

The purpose is to create sufficient marketplace density.

## Step 2 — Recruit Relevant Supply

Build a verified worker base before large-scale customer acquisition.

A marketplace with customer demand but no suitable workers creates poor experiences.

## Step 3 — Start With High-Frequency Categories

Prioritize categories that:

- Are easy to understand
- Have frequent demand
- Can be completed relatively quickly
- Have enough potential workers
- Have manageable safety risk

Potential examples include:

- Plumbing
- Electrical
- Gardening
- Cleaning
- Moving/labour
- General repairs

The exact launch categories require validation.

## Step 4 — Prove the Transaction

Measure whether customers can post jobs and receive useful bids.

## Step 5 — Improve Trust

Use successful completed jobs, ratings, verification, and operational learning to improve marketplace quality.

## Step 6 — Expand Categories

Expand only after marketplace liquidity is proven.

## Step 7 — Expand Geography

Expand city by city rather than attempting immediate nationwide coverage.

The growth principle is:

> **Prove density and successful transactions before expanding complexity.**

# 27. PRODUCT PRINCIPLES

Future GRAFT product decisions should be guided by the following principles.

## 1. Zimbabwe First

Design for Zimbabwean users, payments, behaviour, infrastructure, and operating realities.

## 2. Low Bandwidth First

Assume connectivity may be slow, unstable, or expensive.

## 3. Trust Before Transactions

The marketplace must make users feel safer and more accountable than unstructured alternatives.

## 4. Simple Beats Complicated

Every unnecessary step creates friction for customers and workers.

## 5. EcoCash First

The initial payment direction should focus on the relevant payment ecosystem, subject to capability and compliance validation.

## 6. Location Matters

Nearby relevant workers can improve speed and reduce unnecessary travel, but distance alone must not determine the best match.

## 7. Workers Need Opportunity

GRAFT should create genuine opportunities for skilled people to be discovered and compete.

## 8. Customers Need Control

Customers should generally compare meaningful options and choose rather than being forced into an unnecessary automatic match.

## 9. Privacy by Default

Expose only the personal and location information necessary for the relevant stage of the job.

## 10. Build for Affordable Phones

The product must work beyond flagship devices and high-performance hardware.

## 11. Build for Real Transactions

The goal is successfully completed marketplace jobs, not vanity metrics.

# 32. VISION RISKS AND STRATEGIC GUARDRAILS

The vision is ambitious, but several risks must remain visible.

## Risk: The Marketplace Becomes Too Broad Too Early

**Why:** Supporting too many categories immediately may fragment supply and demand.

**Trade-off:** Broad category ambition versus focused marketplace liquidity.

**Recommendation:** Keep the architecture and category model expandable, but launch with validated high-frequency categories.

---

## Risk: Trust Requirements Create Too Much Onboarding Friction

**Why:** Strong verification can improve trust but may discourage workers if the process is too difficult.

**Trade-off:** Safety and trust versus marketplace supply and onboarding speed.

**Recommendation:** Use an appropriate tiered verification model and validate each requirement.

---

## Risk: Bidding Creates a Race to the Bottom

**Why:** Workers may underbid unsustainably.

**Trade-off:** Customer price choice versus worker earnings and service quality.

**Recommendation:** Present trust, skills, ETA, experience, and quality alongside price.

---

## Risk: Established Workers Dominate

**Why:** Reputation systems can make it difficult for new workers to gain opportunities.

**Trade-off:** Ranking proven workers highly versus fair access for new workers.

**Recommendation:** Build fairness into discovery while maintaining quality and trust standards.

---

## Risk: Payment Assumptions Are Incorrect

**Why:** EcoCash capabilities, settlement, refunds, and compliance may impose constraints.

**Trade-off:** Desired marketplace flow versus validated technical and commercial reality.

**Recommendation:** Validate payment capabilities before finalizing payment architecture.

---

## Risk: Low Connectivity Breaks the Core Experience

**Why:** Heavy applications, live maps, uploads, and tracking can perform poorly.

**Trade-off:** Rich features versus reliable access.

**Recommendation:** Keep low-bandwidth behaviour as a permanent product principle.
